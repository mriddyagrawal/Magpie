# "Letter from Birmingham Jail" by Martin Luther King Jr. (from *What So Proudly We Hail*)

This scanned document presents the complete text of Martin Luther King Jr.'s "Letter from Birmingham Jail," written April 16, 1963, as published in the anthology *What So Proudly We Hail: The American Soul in Story, Speech, and Song*. Composed while King was imprisoned for civil rights demonstrations, the open letter defends nonviolent direct action against white clergy who criticized his tactics as "unwise and untimely." King articulates a moral framework for civil disobedience—distinguishing just laws from unjust laws using theological arguments from St. Augustine and St. Thomas Aquinas—while critiquing the complacency of white moderates, the silence of the organized church, and explaining the four steps of nonviolent campaigns (collection of facts, negotiation, self-purification, and direct action).
