# Analysis of a Flowchart Artwork Depicting Plato's Educational System for Philosopher Rulers

This document examines a visual artwork that uses contemporary flowchart notation—rectangles for learning stages, diamonds for assessments, and parallelograms for endpoints—to interpret the educational curriculum of Plato's Philosopher Ruler. The analysis presents dual readings of the artwork as either mocking the Allegory of the Cave or extending it to illustrate knowledge acquisition, while detailing the meritocratic, non-hereditary structure where students who fail abstract reasoning become Harmon-ists, Astronomers, or lower guardians. The author notes how the artwork addresses textual gaps in Plato's work, particularly the unspecified fate of those who fail Dialectic studies, and draws parallels between this ancient fifty-year education model and modern meritocratic systems such as American liberal arts colleges.
