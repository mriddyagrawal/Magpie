# Analysis of Machiavelli's 'The Prince': Virtu, Fortune, and Political Realism

This document presents an academic analysis of Niccolò Machiavelli's 'The Prince,' focusing on the concepts of virtu (political effectiveness distinct from moral virtue) and fortuna (fortune). The text argues that Machiavelli divorces political theory from ethics, advocating that rulers must prioritize results and state maintenance over conventional morality. It examines how virtu enables rule through glory, respect, and fear, while contending that fortune can be controlled through boldness and adaptability rather than passive caution. The analysis also addresses Machiavelli's empirical approach to different principality types, the inseparability of the prince's fate from that of the kingdom, and the necessity for rulers to be willing to act immorally when required for state preservation.
