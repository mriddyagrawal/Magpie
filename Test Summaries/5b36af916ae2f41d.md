# Machiavelli's The Prince: Analysis of Virtù, Fortuna, and Political Realism

This document comprises academic notes or a draft essay analyzing Niccolò Machiavelli's "The Prince," distinguishing between virtù (practical effectiveness and results) and moral virtue, while examining fortuna as a controllable force rather than an omnipotent driver of affairs. It details Machiavelli's empirical approach to statecraft, covering the conquest and maintenance of monarchies and republics, the necessity of adaptability to changing circumstances, and the separation of political theory from ethics via the "effectual truth." The text emphasizes that rulers must prioritize state preservation and glory through wisdom, well-practiced cruelty, and bold action, citing historical examples including Cesare Borgia and Ramiro de Lorca to illustrate the practical application of power.
