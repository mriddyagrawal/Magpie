# Letter from Birmingham Jail by Martin Luther King Jr. (from "What So Proudly We Hail")

This document presents the full text of Martin Luther King Jr.'s "Letter from Birmingham Jail," written on April 16, 1963, and published in the anthology "What So Proudly We Hail: The American Soul in Story, Speech, and Song." Composed while imprisoned for civil rights demonstrations, the letter is a response to eight white Alabama clergymen who had publicly criticized King's methods as "unwise and untimely." King systematically defends nonviolent direct action as a necessary means to create crisis and foster negotiation, distinguishes between just laws (which align with moral law) and unjust laws (which degrade human personality) citing authorities from St. Augustine to St. Thomas Aquinas, and expresses profound disappointment with the "white moderate" and the white church for prioritizing order over justice. He argues that "injustice anywhere is a threat to justice everywhere," rejecting the call to wait for rights and framing the struggle within America's democratic heritage and Christian ethical tradition.
