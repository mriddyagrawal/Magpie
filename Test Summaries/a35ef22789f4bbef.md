# Orbital Period Changes in Fourteen Novae: Refutation of Standard Evolution Models

This paper reports measurements of sudden orbital period changes (ΔP) across 14 nova eruptions and steady period derivatives (Ṗ) during 20 inter-eruption intervals using eclipse timing data from archival plates and modern surveys (AAVSO, TESS, ZTF). The findings critically refute three cornerstone models of cataclysmic variable (CV) evolution: standard theory (mass-loss dominated) fails completely for five novae with negative ΔP and cannot explain order-of-magnitude variations in U Sco; the Hibernation Model is invalidated because all ΔP values are too small to trigger hibernation and nearly half are negative (anti-hibernation); and the Magnetic Braking Model fails by orders of magnitude for 9 of 13 novae, as observed Ṗ values scatter widely (±10⁻⁹) versus predictions (−10⁻¹³ to −10⁻¹¹). The author concludes that unknown physical mechanisms—possibly asymmetric mass ejection (jetting)—dominate period evolution and must be 100–10,000× more effective than current theory allows.
