# Orbital Period Changes for Fourteen Novae and the Critical Failures of Standard Evolutionary Models

This paper reports measurements of sudden orbital period changes (ΔP) across 14 nova eruptions and steady period derivatives (Ṗ) during 20 inter-eruption intervals for cataclysmic variables (CVs). The author finds that standard theory—which predicts ΔP should be positive and dominated by mass loss—fails completely because five systems exhibit negative ΔP values, and the recurrent nova U Sco shows order-of-magnitude variations in ΔP between photometrically identical eruptions. The Hibernation Model is refuted because observed ΔP values are orders of magnitude too small to trigger accretion drops, with nearly half showing period decreases ('anti-hibernation'). The Magnetic Braking Model (MBM) fails for 9 of 13 novae, with observed Ṗ values scattering over ±10⁻⁹ while predictions range only from -10⁻¹³ to -10⁻¹¹, indicating that unknown physical mechanisms 100–10,000× larger than current theory dominate CV evolution.
