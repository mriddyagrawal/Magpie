# Cataclysmic Variables: A Review of Classification, Evolution, and Multifrequency Properties

This 2012 review by Giovannelli and Sabau-Graziati examines cataclysmic variables (CVs)—binary systems where a white dwarf accretes matter from a low-mass companion—detailing their classification into non-magnetic, intermediate polars (IPCVs), and polars (PCVs) based on magnetic field strength and accretion geometry. The paper synthesizes multifrequency observational data from radio to gamma rays to explain emission mechanisms from components like the accretion disc and boundary layer, while discussing evolutionary models involving the orbital period gap and the 'gravimagnetic rotator' framework. It further explores the connection between recurrent novae and Type Ia supernova progenitors, and identifies open questions regarding outburst mechanisms, the existence of the period gap, and the ambiguous classification of systems like SS Cygni.
