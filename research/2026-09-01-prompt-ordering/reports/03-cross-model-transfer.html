Ordering Doesn’t Transfer

The same 500 questions, the same three arrangements, a different model — and the optimal ordering flips. What gained LFM2.5-VL-3B thirteen points costs Gemma 4 nine. A finding measured on one model is not a fact about prompts.

Gemma 4 26B A4B · bf16-class OpenRouter · DeepInfra pinned LongBench HotpotQA · n=500 greedy · T=0 · seed 0 1500 requests · 6.3 min · $1.22

The finding

For LFM2.5-VL-3B, moving the question after the document was worth +13.6 points. For Gemma 4 26B A4B, the identical change costs −9.2 points.

Both results are strongly significant — p = 1.3×10−8 and p = 7.4×10−6. Same items, same system prompt, same decoding, same anagram guarantee. The two models simply want opposite arrangements.

This is the mechanism. LFM’s question-first collapse — 0.650 to 0.255 — is what made document-first so valuable to it: putting the question next to the generation point rescued a question that was otherwise being stranded 12,000 tokens back. Gemma doesn’t have that problem. Its question-first accuracy is essentially flat across the whole length range, and at 8k+ it is actually higher than at 4–8k.

Once a model can hold the question across long context, the trade reverses: seeing the question before reading the document lets it read for the answer, which is worth more than recency. LFM never gets to collect that benefit because it can’t retain the question that far.

03The confound I can’t remove

Read the cross-model gap with caution

Gemma’s answers average 114 characters; LFM’s average 34. Because the escalation rule sends verbose answers to the LLM judge, 66.7% of Gemma’s answers were judged, against 20.3% of LFM’s. If the judge is even slightly generous toward a fluent sentence that contains the right entity, Gemma collects that benefit three times as often. Some unknown part of the +14 to +17 point cross-model gap is judge leniency rather than comprehension.

This does not affect the ordering findings, which are the point of the report. Within each model, all three arms have identical verbosity, identical escalation rates, and identical judging — so the reversal is clean. It is only the Gemma vs LFM absolute comparison that carries this asymmetry.

Fixing it properly would mean a head-to-head where both models’ answers are judged at the same rate — either by forcing terse output from Gemma, or by judging all 3,000 answers from both. Neither is expensive; neither has been done.

Other limits

  • No token-level verification. Locally I could dump token IDs and prove the arms were anagrams. Through a chat API I can only guarantee identical bytes rearranged — though the provider’s reported input token counts came back identical for STD and SDT on all 500 items, which is strong circumstantial support.
  • Different serving stacks. LFM ran on llama.cpp Q6_K that we built; Gemma ran on DeepInfra’s infrastructure at their quantization. Pinning the provider held quantization constant within the Gemma run (all 1,500 requests served by DeepInfra), but the two models are not on matched hardware.
  • One dataset. HotpotQA multi-hop only. Whether the reversal holds for summarization or single-hop extraction is untested.
  • Two models is not a trend. We now know the effect is not universal. We do not know which of the two is typical.

04What this changes

The headline finding of the previous report is now scoped, not overturned. “Put the question after the document” remains correct for LFM2.5-VL-3B, which is what Magpie ships, and the +13.6 points still stand. What’s gone is any claim that this is a fact about prompting in general.

Prompt-ordering choices must be re-measured whenever the model changes. If Magpie ever swaps its local model — as it already did once, Gemma 4 E4B to LFM2.5-VL-3B in August — the prompt assembly needs re-testing, not porting. Carrying the ordering forward on the assumption that it’s a property of prompts would silently cost around nine points on a Gemma-family model.

The capability gap is real and large. Even discounting for the judging asymmetry, Gemma 4 26B A4B is well clear of LFM2.5-VL-3B at every length, and its worst ordering beats LFM’s best. It is also multimodal and activates only 3.8B parameters per token out of 25.2B — roughly LFM’s active compute. The blocker is the 25.2B of weights that must be resident, not the compute. That makes it a genuine question rather than a closed one: what would it take to ship?

And a cheaper immediate option: Gemma’s robustness to context length is exactly the weakness LFM shows. If Magpie has a cloud escalation path, long documents are precisely where it earns its cost — LFM at 8k+ scores 0.255 question-first, where Gemma scores 0.720.
